requiredTrustProvisions:
  - title: "Customer named as beneficiary; AHCCCS as remainder beneficiary"
    description: "The customer must be designated as the trust's beneficiary. AHCCCS (or \"the State Medicaid Agency\") must be named remainder beneficiary -- for an irrevocable trust, upon the customer's death; for a revocable trust, upon the trust being revoked or terminated, or upon the customer's death, whichever happens first. Arizona is one of the few states that permits either a revocable or an irrevocable Income-Only Trust, provided this remainder-beneficiary rule is met either way."
    agencyCitation: "EPM 803-A"
  - title: "Created by the customer, spouse, parent, or a legal representative"
    description: "The trust must be created by the customer; the customer's parent, if the customer is a minor child; the customer's spouse; or a legal representative, including a court or administrative body with legal authority to act for the customer or spouse."
    agencyCitation: "EPM 803-C"
  - title: "Funded only with income -- no resources"
    description: "The trust corpus can only be the customer's income. Resources (real or personal property, savings, or any other asset that isn't income) cannot be added to or used to fund the trust. If a resource is deposited, the trust loses its special treatment until the resource is removed -- so the trust language must make clear that only income may be added."
    agencyCitation: "EPM 803-C"
  - title: "The full gross amount of the assigned income source, deposited every month"
    description: "This is a whole-source rule, not an excess-only rule: the full gross amount of any income source assigned to the trust must be deposited, in the month the customer receives it. The trust document (or an attached Schedule A) must list the customer's gross income from the assigned source -- it may state that \"gross\" income is assigned rather than listing the dollar amount. No tax withholding, union dues, or life/other-person insurance premiums may be deducted from that income before it reaches the trust; the customer must ask the income source directly to stop those deductions."
    agencyCitation: "EPM 803-C"
  - title: "Direct deposit into an account titled to the trust"
    description: "Income assigned to the trust must be directly deposited, when legally allowed, into a financial account titled to show the trust holds it (for example, \"[Name] Income Only Trust -- [Name] Trustee\"). The account itself must be opened with a $0.00 balance before that first deposit."
    agencyCitation: "EPM 803-A; EPM 803-C; DE-819"
  - title: "Disbursements limited to what state law allows"
    description: "Money can be spent only for the customer's benefit and only for the purposes listed in Arizona Revised Statutes § 36-2934.01. The trust cannot allow the trustee to make loans, gifts, or payments to anyone other than the customer -- even a clause generally authorizing the trustee to make \"loans\" is too vague to comply."
    agencyCitation: "A.R.S. § 36-2934.01; EPM 803-A"
  - title: "Trust expenses require AHCCCS or Probate Court approval"
    description: "The trust must state that it allows disbursements for reasonable and necessary administrative expenses only as approved by AHCCCS, or by the Probate Court with advance notice to AHCCCS."
    agencyCitation: "EPM 803-A"
  - title: "References Arizona and the federal authority correctly"
    description: "The trust cannot replace every reference to Arizona, ALTCS, or AHCCCS with a parallel reference to another state's Medicaid agency -- a real risk if a template drafted for a different state is reused. It must also reference 42 U.S.C. § 1396p(d) (or Section 1917(d)(4) of the Social Security Act) as its federal authority."
    agencyCitation: "EPM 803-A"
  - title: "Combined income can't exceed the local Private Pay Rate"
    description: "For the trust to qualify for special treatment, the customer's counted income that is NOT assigned to the trust, plus the income that IS assigned, must together be at or below the private-pay rate for the geographic area where the customer lives. An undue-hardship exception can be requested case-by-case when the private-pay rate genuinely isn't enough to meet the customer's needs, but it is not automatic."
    agencyCitation: "EPM 803-C"

uplNotes: "Arizona has no current standalone UPL statute -- Ariz. Rev. Stat. § 32-261 (the old criminal UPL provision) was repealed in 1985 after a political fight over realtors preparing purchase contracts (Jonathan Rose, Unauthorized Practice of Law in Arizona, 34 Ariz. St. L.J. 585 (2002)). UPL is instead governed by Arizona Supreme Court Rule 31, which defines \"practice of law\" as providing legal advice or services \"to or for another\" by preparing a document intended to affect legal rights \"for a specific person or entity,\" expressing legal opinions, representing another in a proceeding, or negotiating rights for a specific person -- verified verbatim from two State Bar of Arizona UPL Advisory Opinions (06-02, 12-01) that quote the rule in full. Rule 31 also operates alongside Arizona Code of Judicial Administration § 7-208, which affirmatively authorizes Certified Legal Document Preparers to make legal forms and documents available to the public and provide general legal information (not advice) -- a built-in, sanctioned lane for nonlawyer form/information distribution that most states don't have this explicitly. The seminal Arizona UPL case, State Bar of Arizona v. Arizona Land Title & Trust Co., 90 Ariz. 76, 366 P.2d 1 (1961), held that title companies and realtors could not draft instruments for parties to a transaction -- a \"preparing documents for another\" holding, not a self-help-kit or forms-publisher case, and the public backlash against it is part of what led to the 1985 statute repeal. No Arizona Supreme Court case, ethics opinion, or UPL Advisory Committee opinion addressing Medicaid planning, trust kits, or the Dacey forms-publishing fact pattern was found in this pass -- a search across the full list of published azbar.org UPL Advisory Opinions turned up HOA management, mechanic's liens, out-of-state attorneys, paralegal titles, and tax representatives, but nothing on trusts or estate/Medicaid planning. Net read: Arizona's UPL posture toward nonlawyer forms activity is ordinary-to-permissive, the opposite direction from Florida's SC14-211 advisory opinion (which specifically named \"Qualified Income Trust forms or kits\") -- this is the same 'no on-point adverse holding' posture as Oklahoma, not a unique advantage, and the product stays defensible by holding the same four hard rules regardless: explain what AHCCCS's policy requires, never draft or provide sample/model trust language, never select or tailor language to an individual's facts, and never review a customer's own drafted trust. Confirm Arizona UPL comfort before go-live; this search was not exhaustive."
